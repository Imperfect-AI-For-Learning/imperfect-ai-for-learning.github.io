Motivation & Goals

Workshop Overview

Generative AI introduces a transformative paradigm for everyday learning, work, and sensemaking. Yet instead of viewing imperfections solely as obstacles to eliminate, we examine how AI errors can provoke productive inquiry.

"Instead of designing AI that never gets things wrong, how might we design interactions in which being wrong — at the right moment, in the right way — helps humans learn?"

— Workshop Central Organizing Question

The Conventional Paradigm vs. The New Possibility

Generative AI systems produce unpredictable errors: factual hallucinations, subtle biases, inconsistent reasoning, contextual misunderstandings, and unwarranted confidence. Conventionally, Human-AI Interaction research has focused on eliminating errors to make systems trustworthy, predictable, and accurately relied upon.

However, learning science research shows that error-free learning is often pedagogically sterile. Erroneous worked examples, productive failure, and negative knowledge development demonstrate that encountering errors stimulates cognitive conflict, self-explanation, and deeper metacognitive awareness.

From Learning 'Despite' AI to Learning 'Through' AI

Generative AI is not merely a static textbook. It can dynamically generate productive ambiguity, alternative explanations, and flawed reasoning on demand. Frameworks like AI fallibility-as-resource (AiFAR) and the pedagogy of AI mistakes shift our focus from tolerating AI flaws to purposefully scaffolding critical evaluation.

Crucially, imperfect AI is not inherently productive. Unscaffolded errors cause misconceptions, overreliance, or frustration. This workshop gathers researchers to establish when and how AI imperfections stimulate positive learning outcomes.

Four Core Workshop Goals

1

Characterize Design Space

Identify different forms of AI imperfections (hallucination, biased reasoning, logic bugs, overconfidence) and the specific learning contexts where they become pedagogically relevant.

2

Cognitive Mechanisms

Unpack cognitive and pedagogical mechanisms (comparison, contrast, cognitive dissonance, curiosity, self-explanation) that distinguish productive learning from confusing friction.

3

Scaffolded Interactions

Co-design interactive systems and pedagogical scaffolds that prompt learners to notice, interrogate, verify, and reflect upon flawed AI outputs without falling prey to misinformation.

4

Future-Resilient Principles

Formulate learning design principles that withstand rapid advances in model capabilities, accounting for both transient limitations and persistent structural human-data biases.

Call for Participation

Submissions & Participation

We welcome researchers, educators, interaction designers, and practitioners from Human-AI Interaction, Learning Sciences, AI in Education, AI Literacy, and Responsible AI.

Who Should Join?

We invite contributions envisioning the role of AI imperfections in learning and sensemaking across broad interdisciplinary areas:

  • Human-AI Interaction (HCI): Novel interactive scaffolds, trust calibration interfaces, and feedback designs.
  • Learning Sciences & AIED: Cognitive mechanisms of productive failure, negative knowledge, and dialogic learning.
  • Critical AI Literacy: Empowering diverse learners to recognize bias, hallucination, and limitations in AI systems.
  • Explainable & Responsible AI: Uncertainty communication, fallibility disclosure, and ethical boundary conditions.

Submission Requirements

Participants are invited to submit a 2–4 pages position paper (excluding references) using the CHI Extended Abstract format or ACM Master Article Template.

Your submission must include:

  1. Research / Position Narrative: Description of completed or ongoing work, distinctive conceptual perspective, or open research direction addressing how imperfect AI supports or hinders learning.
  2. One Concrete Imperfect AI Example: An artifact, prompt-response pair, or observed interaction drawn from classroom, research, design, or everyday experience for the Imperfect AI Gallery.

* At least one author of each accepted submission must register and attend the workshop in person. Capacity is planned for 20–25 registered participants.

Publication & Dissemination

With authors' permission, accepted position papers will be published open-access on this workshop website repository prior to CHI 2027 to spark discussion.

Collaborative Synthesis Paper

Schedule & Format

Workshop Program

A three-hour highly interactive, co-design focused format structured across two 90-minute sessions with provocation talks, small-group analysis, gallery walks, and collaborative synthesis.

Session #1: Foundations & The Imperfect AI Gallery

From identifying AI fallibility to examining learner cognitive responses

Part 1 • 90 min
00:00 – 00:30
30 mins

Welcome & Invited Opening Talk

Welcome from organizers followed by an invited talk from an expert in the Learning Sciences / AIED addressing when and under what conditions encountering errors, discrepancies, or difficulties promotes deeper learning.
00:30 – 00:50
20 mins

Participant Lightning Talks

Rapid-fire 90-second presentations where participants introduce their distinctive disciplinary perspectives through a concrete example of an imperfect AI response (incomplete, inconsistent, misleading, or unexpected).
00:50 – 01:20
30 mins

Activity 1: Imperfect AI Gallery

Participants circulate in small groups through curated case boards displayed across the room, analyzing real-world flawed AI interactions guided by three key questions:
  • What is imperfect about the AI response or interaction?
  • What might a learner notice, question, or do in response?
  • What, if anything, could be learned through engaging with this imperfection?
01:20 – 01:30
10 mins

Break & Informal Discussion

Networking and informal continuation of conversations around the Imperfect AI Gallery exhibits.

Session #2: Mechanisms, Co-Design & Research Agenda

Translating insights into interactive systems and a sustainable research agenda

Part 2 • 90 min
01:30 – 02:00
30 mins

Activity 2: From Imperfection to Learning Opportunity

Deep-dive small group analysis mapping cases through three lenses: (1) learner behavioral reactions; (2) activated cognitive mechanisms (comparison, cognitive conflict, curiosity); and (3) learning outcomes. Groups also establish critical boundary conditions when errors become harmful misinformation.
02:00 – 02:30
30 mins

Activity 3: Designing for Productive Imperfection

Hands-on interactive co-design in interdisciplinary teams. Groups design a prototype interaction or pedagogical workflow that scaffolds learners in productively interrogating AI fallibility while actively mitigating overreliance.
02:30 – 02:55
25 mins

Activity 4: Building a Sustainable Research Agenda

Collective synthesis: each group formulates pressing open questions and future-resilient design principles that account for both current AI limitations and next-generation model capabilities.
02:55 – 03:00
5 mins

Closing Remarks & Community Next Steps

Wrap-up of findings, archiving workshop artifacts, and organizing co-authorship for the post-workshop collaborative synthesis article.
